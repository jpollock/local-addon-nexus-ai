# Companion density · final

*M4 at companion rank · 380px docked · supersedes RB-C and RB-D turn 1*

The ruled composite: 1a's layout — the declared block pinned to the top of the session, folding in place when the run finishes — with 1b's empty run, where no container opens at all. Sibling of the stage density; every fact on this sheet comes from the same derived fixture, so a difference between the two is a defect in one of them rather than a density choice.

Three panels, all at the shipped 380px: **waiting on you**, **finished — folded in place**, **the empty run**.

## The panels

**Waiting on you.** While a decision is pending the declaration yields to a digest: its facts in full — runbook, reference, provable denominator, scope — and its checkpoint list windowed on the gate being decided (`Checkpoints 2 to 4 of 8`), with the full declaration one promotion away at stage rank. The approval card sits in the flow, where she is reading. No inner scrollbar anywhere in the block.

**Finished — folded in place.** One row where the block was, still at the top of the session, with the record openable in place. The conversation has the column back.

**The empty run.** No container opens. The refusal stays a turn, the derived plan attaches verbatim (`rb.bulk-plugin-update · v1.2.0 · marked strict · cp.dry-run — 0 cells eligible`), and the alternatives are offers.

## The pins this density owes

- The block yields to the card, never the reverse: while an approval is pending the declaration renders as a digest sized to what the card leaves. The decision outranks the declaration while the run is waiting on one.
- The digest carries every fact and no scrollbar: runbook and reference, the provable denominator, the scope block's facts, and a window of the checkpoint list centred on the gate, with the window's range stated. The full declaration is one promotion away — at stage rank, where it fits.
- The run folds in place on finish — one row where the block was, at the top of the session. The record does not move, so what she read before the run is what she reopens after it.
- The runbook reference appears exactly once, in the declared block's header. Not in the card, not in the session header, not in a turn.
- The badge appears only on the checkpoints the runbook contributed, read from the derived data — approval and backup carry none.
- The scope block's facts are byte-invariant across densities, from the one fixture object: the headline, the places line, every group head with its count, the door, and the from-line.
- At 380px the explanation is deferred, not rewritten — cell lists, reasons and the from-line sit behind a disclosure, while the barred group's head and door stay on screen. A density may defer explanation; it may never defer a fact, and never the door that names what would make a refusal a yes.
- A plan of zero cells opens no container: the refusal stays a turn, the derived plan attaches verbatim, and the alternatives are offers.
- Three marks only — attested, recorded-not-proved, not-yet. The tick means attested and may never grow a "verified" in a caption, tooltip or hover.

## What is absent, and on purpose

- No run-mode bar. Rank is promotion, not navigation; a mode you can leave is a mode you can lose track of, and the bar was doing navigation's work.
- No not-applicable dash. It died with the armed empty block that needed it — a fourth mark meaning nothing-attests is vocabulary by drift.
- No attest shortforms. The full words render at 380px; a shortform set would be a second wording of the sentence the seam exists to keep single.
- No invented chrome: the tabs are the ones the addon ships, and the site strip is where it ships. A screen that claims chrome the product lacks cannot argue for changing the chrome it has.
- No authored procedure. Checkpoint order, attest classes, attest wording, labels, badges, version and denominator come from `declared-procedures.json`; only scenario narrative is local.

## J-Refusal · the spec section this document owns

*Authored here, so the register's blocked section lifts to this text.*

The journey runs: an act needs authority it does not have, the refusal names what would make it yes, the widening is made where widenings are made, and the work resumes where it stopped. Written against the two refusal states drawn on the sheet — the empty run, where the world's state is the answer, and the split scope, where a grant is.

**Key steps**

- The refusal is a turn, and the derived plan it produced attaches to it verbatim — the reason is inspectable, not asserted.
- The refusal names what would make it yes: the missing capability in the vocabulary the Settings matrix uses, or the world-state that must change first.
- Where a grant is the answer, the door renders on the barred group in the scope block and deep-links to that specific grant — not to the top of Settings.
- Where the world-state is the answer, the alternatives render as offers, including the one that narrows the ask to what may run now.
- A partly-authorized selection splits rather than refuses: the runnable subset arms, the barred subset states its reason and its door.
- Crossing into Settings and back resumes the same session with the act still armed and its scope intact; the widening is recorded as a control event, visible and revocable.

**Must not**

- A container for a refused run — a plan of zero cells earns no rank, so it draws no checkpoint list.
- A refusal that says no without naming what would have made it yes.
- A conversational shortcut that elicits the widening in the chat that walked her there.
- A re-ask of anything the session already established, on either side of the excursion.
- A silent trim of the selection down to whatever happens to be permitted.
- An offer that would break the rule the refusal just enforced — starting a halted site in order to update it is not an alternative, it is the refusal reworded.

## Two restatements of ratified pins, for your ruling

Both came out of building the composite at the real width, and both change a pin you have already ratified, so neither is mine to adopt alone.

**1 · The yield rule, restated as a digest.** As ratified it read: while an approval card is up the block gives back space and holds 200px. At 380px that number cannot be satisfied — the block's own header is 101px and the collapsed scope band is another 152px, so no checkpoint row can be visible at any scroll position, and the rule's own purpose (the declaration stays on screen while the run produces evidence against it) fails while its letter is obeyed. Restated: the declaration yields to a digest sized to what the card leaves — every fact, a stated window of the checkpoint list centred on the gate, no scrollbar, and the full declaration one promotion away. This is §0 §2 read literally rather than approximately: at this width the declaration and the decision cannot both be *fully* visible, so the declaration yields.

**2 · Byte-identity, restated as fact-identity.** As ratified, pin 2 said all of the scope block's text is identical across densities. At 380px the three groups' reasons need 426px, more than twice the block's whole viewport. Restated: the block's *facts* are byte-invariant across densities — headline, places, every group head with its count, the door, the from-line — while the *explanation* (cell lists, reasons) may be deferred behind a disclosure at companion rank. The barred group's head and door never defer, because that pair is J-Refusal's naming. If you would rather hold the original pin, the alternative is that the companion never renders a scope block at all and the handoff shows only at stage — which I would argue against, since the barred door is the most useful thing on the panel.
