# Selection becomes scope — the split case

*Draft 1's fixture drew a scope that rb.bulk-plugin-update refuses at pre.no-production. That refusal is the state worth designing: a selection whose consequence exceeds its authority, split in the block rather than at a gate.*

The declaration is the canonical eight checkpoints in canonical order, with the shipped attest words and the authored `unrequested` badges. Nothing on this sheet is an authored procedure.

## 1 · Inspect · the comparator render

**She selects five behind cells, and two of them are production.** The selection is honest about the fleet and says nothing about authority. Nothing here should stop her selecting a production cell — the matrix is a shape, not a permission model.

## 2 · The artifact that crosses · split state

**The scope block splits itself by law, before anything arms.** Three groups, each stating its reason: what runs under the grant that exists, what is barred and by which precondition, what is excluded by the world's own state. The barred group carries the door — J-Refusal arriving inside the handoff instead of at a gate.

- **Runs now · 3 cells** — Alpha · staging, Delta · staging, Echo · staging. Within this runbook's declared environments and the grant you have made.
- **Needs a grant · 2 cells** — Bravo · production, Charlie · production. rb.bulk-plugin-update declares local, staging and development only; pre.no-production refuses production outright. Production needs a separate grant and its own version of this runbook, which does not exist yet. → *Review what agents may do → plugin updates on production*
- **Excluded · 1 site** — Foxtrot · staging. Halted 14h, and said so. Nothing about authority — the world's own state.

*From your matrix selection, 12:04 · WooCommerce row, behind-newest filter*

### The rules the split follows

- The split is derived from the runbook's declared environments and the grant list, not from a check the surface performs — the same refusal the arming would have produced, rendered before she spends a decision on it.
- A barred cell is never silently dropped and never blocks the runnable subset. Refusing the whole selection because two cells exceed authority is the trimming we ruled against.
- The barred group names the capability in the vocabulary the Settings matrix uses, so the door lands on that grant rather than on the top of Settings.
- Ordering inside the block is by authority, not by place: what runs, what needs permission, what the world removed.

### What is deliberately absent

- No authored checkpoint order: the eight lines and their attest words come from the runbook document and the seam.
- No turn restating the scope in prose, in either density.
- No confirm-your-targets step — the set is the one she selected, minus what law removed, each removal stated.
- No refusal of the whole selection because part of it exceeds authority.
- No place named as a single value, and no silent exclusion anywhere.

### On the marks

No new vocabulary proposed. The attest words render in full at both densities — a shortform mark set would be a second wording of the one sentence the seam exists to keep single, and the words are shorter than the checkpoint titles they sit under.

## Pins · the five inherited, plus three the split adds

- The dry run's target cells equal the selected cells minus the barred and excluded sets, asserted as sets, in both densities.
- The scope block's rendered text is identical across the selection bar, the companion declaration and the stage document.
- Every line is derived: cells from the selection, places from the affected-set derivation the ambient order sorts by, bars from the runbook's declared environments and the grant list, exclusions from the record that caused them.
- The from-line resolves to the comparator render that produced the selection, with the same filter applied.
- The arming carries the scope: a run that re-derives its own targets fails this journey.
- A barred group renders the capability id the Settings matrix uses, and its door resolves to that grant.
- The declared list and its attest words come from the canonical document — a checkpoint sequence authored anywhere, including in a design fixture, is a defect.
- The runnable subset arms whether or not a barred subset exists; the two never depend on each other.

## The question this draft asked, since ruled

When the barred subset is granted later, does the original session resume with the full five, or does the widening start a second run under the production runbook version? My reading was the second — a different runbook is a different procedure, and the walk is an Investigate-style handoff rather than a resumption. Ruled in favour of the second: runs are hash-pinned, approval does not stretch to a different plan, and continuity lives in the provenance — both runs share the from-line that produced them.
