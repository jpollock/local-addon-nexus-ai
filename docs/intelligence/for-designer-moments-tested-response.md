## 3 · Your question, answered: the consequence order

You asked how two consequences compare, and you are right that every
needs-you row is already secretly using an answer. Here it is written
down, derived from the doctrine we already hold rather than invented:
rank by WHAT THE DELAY COSTS AND WHO CAN PAY IT.

**Tier 1 — the world is mid-change and only you can move it.** Abort
halts (completed sites standing, the fleet part-changed), and — once
the incident producer lands — open incidents. A half-changed world ages
badly; every hour it stands is exposure, and the platform is forbidden
to move it (that is what the halt IS).
**Tier 2 — the platform awaits your consent; the world is untouched.**
Approval gates. Untouched worlds keep; the cost of delay is only
deferred work. This tier ordering is the same distinction as "abort
stops future work; it does not undo done work" — a waiting approval and
a standing halt are different DEBTS, and the half-changed one
compounds.
**Tier 3 — the record is going blind.** Producers DARK, folds lagging,
health degraded. Epistemic cost, and it compounds: every minute dark
widens what no verdict can be trusted about. Special rule, from the
silent-failure doctrine: tier 3 can never interrupt, and can never be
sorted below the fold — going-blind may not be scrolled out of sight.
**Tier 4 — changed.** Finished runs, outcomes to review. The cost is
already paid; the record is already filed (J-Return's must).
**Tier 5 — drifting.** Facts past their freshness SLO. Lowest, because
the gateway already fences action on stale facts — drift is annoying,
not dangerous, precisely because the freshness constraint exists.

Within a tier: the PLACE touched orders first (production-touching
above staging above local — the environment gates already encode
consequence-of-place), then age, oldest first (longer accrual). Every
input is derived — the cursor's pending gate, the abort's groups, the
health verdicts, the SLO table — so the row's sort is a computed
verdict end to end, never taste. And the arbitration boundary stands
exactly where objection 3 put it: the order governs the AMBIENT list
and the escalation intensity only; the column changes hands solely by
the user's promotion. The comparator makes the top of the list the
right thing; the human decides to look. The order is written into the
model (1.2, §4a) as v0 law; when policy overrides exist it becomes a
policy document like the SLOs, inspectable and org-tunable — same
pattern as your Q9, same answer.
